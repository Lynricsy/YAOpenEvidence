### Relevance (1)
### Study design  (type, population, n, intervention/comparator, follow-up)
- **Type**: Narrative review of cardiovascular outcome trials (CVOTs), meta-analyses, and mechanistic studies.
- **Population**: Patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM).
- **n**: Not explicitly stated for the review, but individual trials included in the review had varying sample sizes (e.g., SUSTAIN-6: ~3,200 patients; LEADER: ~10,000 patients; REWIND: ~10,000 patients).
- **Intervention/Comparator**: GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) such as semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, and albiglutide compared to placebo.
- **Follow-up**: Varies by trial (e.g., SUSTAIN-6: ~2 years; REWIND: ~5 years).

### Key findings relevant to the question  (bullets; include exact numbers: HR/RR/OR with 95% CI, p, absolute rates; quote short phrases)
- **SUSTAIN-6 trial**: Semaglutide reduced the risk of nonfatal stroke by 39% (HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.38–0.99, p = 0.04) compared to placebo.
- **REWIND trial**: Dulaglutide reduced nonfatal stroke by 24% (HR 0.76; 95% CI, 0.61–0.95; p = 0.017) compared to placebo.
- **LEADER trial**: Liraglutide showed no significant reduction in total stroke (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.71–1.06) or nonfatal stroke (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.72–1.11).
- **HARMONY Outcomes trial**: Albiglutide did not demonstrate a significant effect on fatal or nonfatal stroke (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.66–1.14).
- **PIONEER trial**: Oral semaglutide showed a non-significant but favorable trend toward reduced nonfatal stroke (HR 0.77; 95% CI, 0.56–1.05).
- **ELIXA trial**: Lixisenatide showed no reduction in stroke incidence in patients with acute coronary syndrome.

### Limitations stated by the authors
- **Heterogeneity in trial design and population characteristics**: Variability in patient risk profiles, follow-up duration, treatment exposure, and endpoint definitions contributes to inconsistent results.
- **Limited power for stroke-specific endpoints**: Stroke events were often rare, leading to wide confidence intervals and limited precision in effect estimates.
- **Subgroup findings are hypothesis-generating**: Results from prespecified subgroups (e.g., based on eGFR, baseline cardiovascular disease) are not definitive and require further validation.
- **Commercial withdrawal of some drugs**: For example, albiglutide was withdrawn for commercial reasons, which may affect clinical uptake and interpretation of results.
- **Lack of data in non-diabetic or high-risk populations**: The review highlights a need for targeted studies in non-diabetic or high-risk populations for stroke prevention.

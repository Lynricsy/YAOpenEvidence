### Study design
- **Type**: Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs)
- **Population**: Non-diabetic adults with obesity (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m²)
- **n**: 3,553 participants across 4 RCTs
- **Intervention/Comparator**: Tirzepatide (5-15 mg or maximum tolerated dose (MTD)) vs. placebo
- **Follow-up**: Minimum duration of 52 weeks (up to 72 weeks)

### Key findings relevant to the question
- **Weight loss**: Tirzepatide significantly reduced body weight compared to placebo (mean difference -16.54%; 95% CI -17.48 to -15.59; p < 0.00001), with a clear dose-response relationship (meta-regression β = -0.72% per 1 mg increase; p = 0.0014).
- **Weight-loss thresholds**: Participants receiving tirzepatide were more likely to achieve ≥15% weight loss (OR 23.25; 95% CI 18.06-29.94).
- **BMI reduction**: Tirzepatide reduced BMI by -7.09 kg/m² (95% CI -7.48 to -6.70; p < 0.00001).
- **Waist circumference**: Tirzepatide reduced waist circumference by -12.77 cm (95% CI -13.66 to -11.89; p < 0.00001).
- **HbA1c**: Tirzepatide reduced HbA1c by -0.42% (95% CI -0.44 to -0.40; p < 0.00001).
- **Quality of life**: Improvements in SF-36v2 scores (MD 2.30 points; 95% CI 1.84 to 2.75; p < 0.00001) and IWQOL-Lite-CT scores (MD 12.37 points; 95% CI 9.75-14.99; p < 0.00001).
- **Safety**: Gastrointestinal adverse events (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea) were more frequent with tirzepatide (OR 4.20, 6.93, 3.80, respectively), but the rate of serious adverse events was comparable to placebo (OR 0.97).

### Limitations stated by the authors
- **Heterogeneity**: Substantial heterogeneity was observed in some outcomes (e.g., I² = 85% for percent weight change).
- **Missing outcome data**: Some studies had missing outcome data, leading to an overall risk of bias rating of "some concerns" for SURMOUNT-1 and SURMOUNT-3.
- **Residual heterogeneity**: Some variability in outcomes was not fully explained by dose alone, even after meta-regression analysis.
- **Limited number of studies**: The small number of studies included limited the ability to perform formal statistical analyses for publication bias (e.g., Egger’s regression).

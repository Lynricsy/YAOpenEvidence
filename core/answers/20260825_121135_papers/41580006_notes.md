### Study design  
Type: Meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs)  
Population: Non-diabetic adults with overweight or obesity (BMI ≥27 kg/m²)  
n: 5411 participants across 7 RCTs  
Intervention/Comparator: Semaglutide (oral and subcutaneous) vs. placebo  
Follow-up: Extended follow-up duration (exact duration not specified, but data from trials up to April 30, 2024)

### Key findings relevant to the question  
- Semaglutide significantly reduced absolute weight by **-12.24 kg (95% CI -13.25 to -11.22)**.  
- Percentage weight change was reduced by **-12.15% (95% CI -13.63 to -10.67)**.  
- BMI decreased by **-4.32 kg/m² (95% CI -4.75 to -3.89)**.  
- Waist circumference decreased by **-9.32 cm (95% CI -9.87 to -8.78)**.  
- The likelihood of achieving **≥5% weight loss** was **2.63 times higher (95% CI 2.12–3.25)** with semaglutide.  
- Total adverse events were **1.05 times higher (95% CI 1.01–1.09)**, primarily gastrointestinal symptoms.  
- Serious adverse reactions were **1.33 times higher (95% CI 1.08–1.63)** with semaglutide.  
- Optimal outcomes were observed with **doses ≥2.4 mg weekly** in combination with lifestyle interventions.

### Limitations stated by the authors  
- Comprehensive evidence on long-term efficacy and safety remains limited.  
- The study relies on data from published RCTs, which may not capture all adverse events or long-term outcomes.  
- Heterogeneity across trials may affect the generalizability of results.  
- The study did not specify the exact duration of follow-up in individual trials.

### Relevance
3

### Study design
- **Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Population:** Non-diabetic adults with obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m²)
- **n:** 23,467 participants across 16 trials
- **Intervention/Comparator:** GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) including semaglutide, liraglutide, and tirzepatide, compared to placebo, lifestyle intervention, or other weight loss medications
- **Follow-up:** Median of 68 weeks (range 28-208 weeks)

### Key findings relevant to the question
- **Cardiovascular outcomes:** GLP-1 RAs reduced composite major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) by **20% (RR 0.80, 95% CI 0.72-0.89, p < 0.001)**.
  - **Non-fatal stroke:** **28% reduction (RR 0.72, 95% CI 0.61-0.85, p < 0.001)**
  - **Non-fatal myocardial infarction:** **16% reduction (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.73-0.97, p = 0.02)**
  - **Cardiovascular death:** **15% reduction (RR 0.85, 95% CI 0.73-0.99, p = 0.04)**
  - **All-cause mortality:** **12% reduction (RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.78-0.99, p = 0.03)**
- **Heart failure hospitalization:** **18% reduction (RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.70-0.96, p = 0.01)**
  - **HFpEF (heart failure with preserved ejection fraction):** **27% reduction (RR 0.73, 95% CI 0.58-0.92, p = 0.008)**
- **Weight loss:** **12.4% weight loss (95% CI 11.0-13.8%)**
- **Cardiovascular risk factors:**
  - **Systolic blood pressure:** **4.2 mmHg reduction (95% CI −5.1 to −3.3, p < 0.001)**
  - **Triglycerides:** **32 mg/dL reduction (95% CI −41 to −23, p < 0.001)**
  - **hsCRP (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein):** **38.6% reduction (95% CI −2.4 to −1.2, p < 0.001)**
- **Mediation analysis:** **35%-55% of cardiovascular benefit was independent of weight loss**, with contributions from blood pressure reduction (20%-28%), lipid improvements (15%-22%), inflammatory markers (18%-25%), and unexplained direct vascular effects (15%-25%).

### Limitations stated by the authors
- **Heterogeneity in study populations and outcomes:** Some heterogeneity was observed in the analysis, particularly in baseline cardiovascular disease status.
- **Industry sponsorship:** All included trials were industry-sponsored, which may introduce potential biases.
- **Limited data on certain outcomes:** Some outcomes, such as atrial fibrillation, showed non-significant trends, and data on long-term safety beyond the follow-up period was not available.
- **Sex-specific analyses:** While the study included a large proportion of women (63.8%), the generalizability of findings to other populations may be limited.

3

### Study design  
Type: Narrative review comparing real-world data with randomized controlled trials (RCTs)  
Population: Patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D) and non-T2D (non-diabetic obese patients)  
n: 127 RCTs (58,976 participants) and 26 real-world studies  
Intervention/comparator: Incretin-based therapies, including semaglutide and tirzepatide  
Follow-up: Varies; real-world data includes 6–12 months for weight loss and cardiovascular outcomes

### Key findings relevant to the question  
- In non-diabetic populations, real-world persister analyses (SHAPE study) showed semaglutide achieved -14.1% weight loss and tirzepatide -16.5% weight loss, approximating per-protocol RCT estimates (semaglutide -14.9%, tirzepatide 15 mg -20.9%).  
- Intention-to-treat real-world estimates were substantially lower than per-protocol estimates.  
- Only 13% of semaglutide users reached the target dose of 2.4 mg, and 25.9% of tirzepatide users reached 15 mg.  
- Real-world cardiovascular data showed 20%-46% reductions in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), consistent with RCT results.  
- High discontinuation rates (20%-50% within 1 year) and prior GLP-1 RA exposure were associated with the observed efficacy gap.  
- Clinicians can expect RCT-level outcomes in patients who tolerate and persist with target doses.

### Limitations stated by the authors  
- The comparison is descriptive, not analytical.  
- Differences between T2D and non-T2D populations may reflect channeling bias and differential follow-up.  
- Head-to-head cardiovascular outcome trials comparing tirzepatide and semaglutide in non-diabetic populations are needed.

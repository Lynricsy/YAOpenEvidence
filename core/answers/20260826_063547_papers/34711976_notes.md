### Study design  
- **Type**: Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial  
- **Population**: 324 patients with HFpEF (LVEF ≥ 45%) with NYHA class II–IV symptoms, elevated natriuretic peptides, and requiring diuretic therapy  
- **n**: 324 patients (162 randomized to dapagliflozin, 162 to placebo)  
- **Intervention/Comparator**: Dapagliflozin 10 mg once daily vs. placebo  
- **Follow-up**: 12 weeks  

### Key findings relevant to the question  
- **KCCQ-CS (primary endpoint)**: Dapagliflozin improved KCCQ-CS by 5.8 points (95% CI 2.3–9.2, *P* = 0.001) at 12 weeks.  
- **KCCQ-TS (symptoms)**: Improved by 5.8 points (95% CI 2.0–9.6, *P* = 0.003).  
- **KCCQ-PL (physical limitations)**: Improved by 5.3 points (95% CI 0.7–10.0, *P* = 0.026).  
- **6MWT (secondary endpoint)**: Dapagliflozin improved 6MWT by 20.1 m (95% CI 5.6–34.7, *P* = 0.007).  
- **KCCQ-OS (secondary endpoint)**: Improved by 4.5 points (95% CI 1.1–7.8, *P* = 0.009).  
- **Weight loss**: Dapagliflozin reduced weight by 0.72 kg (95% CI 0.01–1.42, *P* = 0.046).  
- **Responder analysis**: Dapagliflozin increased the odds of a ≥5-point improvement in KCCQ-OS by 1.73 (95% CI 1.05–2.85, *P* = 0.03).  

### Limitations stated by the authors  
- **Short follow-up duration**: The 12-week follow-up does not allow assessment of the durability of the observed benefits on HF-related symptoms or functional status.  
- **Geographic limitation**: All patients were enrolled in the United States, which may limit generalizability to other populations.  
- **No effect on natriuretic peptides**: Dapagliflozin did not significantly affect natriuretic peptides, which are predictive of prognosis in HFpEF.

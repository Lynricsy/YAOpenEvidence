### Study design  
Type: Systematic review and meta-analysis  
Population: Patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF: ≥50%) or mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF: 41-49%)  
n: 15,769 patients across six RCTs  
Intervention/Comparator: SGLT2 inhibitors vs. placebo  
Follow-up: Not explicitly stated, but based on RCTs and post-hoc analyses

### Key findings relevant to the question  
- Pooled analysis showed that SGLT2i use was significantly associated with improved cardiovascular death (CVD) and/or hospitalization for heart failure (HHF) outcomes in HFmrEF/HFpEF (pooled HR 0.80, 95% CI: 0.74, 0.86, p < 0.001, I² = 0%).  
- In patients with HFpEF (N = 8891), SGLT2i use was associated with a significant reduction in CVD/HHF outcomes (HR 0.79, 95% CI: 0.71, 0.87, p < 0.001, I² = 0%).  
- In patients with HFmrEF (N = 4555), SGLT2i use was also associated with a significant reduction in CVD/HHF outcomes (HR 0.77, 95% CI: 0.67, 0.89, p < 0.001, I² = 40%).  
- In HFmrEF/HFpEF subgroup without baseline diabetes (N = 6507), SGLT2i use was associated with a significant reduction in CVD/HHF outcomes (HR 0.80, 95% CI: 0.70, 0.91, p < 0.001, I² = 0%).  
- Sensitivity analysis including the DELIVER and EMPEROR-Preserved trials found a trend towards significant beneficial effects on CV deaths (HR 0.90, 95% CI: 0.79, 1.02, p = 0.08, I² = 0%).

### Study design
The paper is a narrative review that discusses the cardiovascular effects of metformin in both diabetic and non-diabetic populations. It includes data from observational studies, meta-analyses, and clinical trials. For non-diabetic populations, it references subgroup analyses and trials with surrogate endpoints, as well as the ongoing VA-IMPACT trial (NCT02915198) which is designed to evaluate whether metformin reduces major cardiovascular events in prediabetic patients with atherosclerotic disease.

### Key findings relevant to the question
- In individuals without diabetes but at high cardiovascular risk (e.g., prediabetes, obesity, metabolic syndrome), evidence is limited, with most data derived from subgroup analyses or trials with surrogate endpoints.
- Metformin may delay the progression from prediabetes to overt diabetes, with a reduction in incident diabetes by approximately one quarter in high-risk adults.
- In the Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) and its long-term follow-up (DPPOS), metformin significantly slowed the progression from prediabetes to T2DM, especially in younger, overweight individuals and women with a history of gestational diabetes.
- Clinical trials and meta-analyses have shown that metformin reduces incident diabetes by approximately one quarter in high-risk adults, with stronger effects observed in younger, overweight individuals, women with prior gestational diabetes, and those treated for longer durations.
- The VA-IMPACT trial (NCT02915198) is ongoing and aims to clarify whether metformin reduces major cardiovascular events in prediabetic patients with atherosclerotic disease.

### Limitations stated by the authors
- Evidence for cardiovascular benefits in non-diabetic populations is more limited, as most data are derived from subgroup analyses or trials with surrogate endpoints.
- Uncertainties remain regarding the long-term cost-effectiveness, optimal dosing strategies, and cardiovascular benefits of metformin in non-diabetic populations.
- Earlier trials in non-diabetic individuals predominantly yielded negative or inconclusive outcomes, showing no meaningful influence on carotid intima-media thickness (cIMT) or surrogate indicators of CVD in patients with coronary artery disease (CAD) undergoing statin therapy.
- No definitive cardiovascular advantage was identified in the general non-diabetic population, possibly due to insufficient statistical power.
- In the DPP/DPPOS trials, although metformin reduced diabetes incidence, it did not result in a statistically significant reduction in major cardiovascular events, likely due to extensive use of statins and antihypertensives, treatment arm crossover, and design-related factors.

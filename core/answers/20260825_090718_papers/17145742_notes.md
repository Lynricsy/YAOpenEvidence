### Relevance (1)
### Study design  (type, population, n, intervention/comparator, follow-up)
The study is a double-blind, randomized, controlled clinical trial involving 4360 patients with recently diagnosed type 2 diabetes. Participants were randomized to receive rosiglitazone, metformin, or glyburide monotherapy and were treated for a median of 4.0 years. The primary outcome was the time to monotherapy failure, and secondary outcomes included cardiovascular events.

### Key findings relevant to the question  (bullets; include exact numbers: HR/RR/OR with 95% CI, p, absolute rates; quote short phrases)
- Glyburide was associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular events (including congestive heart failure) than rosiglitazone (P<0.05).
- The risk associated with metformin was similar to that with rosiglitazone.
- No specific cardiovascular protection or harm related to metformin was reported in non-diabetic populations, as the study only included patients with type 2 diabetes.

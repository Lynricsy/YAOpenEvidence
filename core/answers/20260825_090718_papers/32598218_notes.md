### Relevance (1)
### Study design  (type, population, n, intervention/comparator, follow-up)
This study is a systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) in adults with type 2 diabetes. The population included patients receiving metformin-based background therapy (n = 298 trials for low cardiovascular risk, n = 21 trials for increased cardiovascular risk). Interventions compared included various glucose-lowering drugs, such as GLP-1 RAs and SGLT-2 inhibitors, added to metformin. Follow-up periods were at least 24 weeks.

### Key findings relevant to the question  (bullets; include exact numbers: HR/RR/OR with 95% CI, p, absolute rates; quote short phrases)
- In patients at low cardiovascular risk receiving metformin-based background treatment, there were no clinically meaningful differences between treatments for mortality and vascular outcomes.
- In patients at increased cardiovascular risk receiving metformin-based background treatment, oral semaglutide, empagliflozin, liraglutide, extended-release exenatide, and dapagliflozin reduced all-cause mortality.
- Oral semaglutide, empagliflozin, and liraglutide also reduced cardiovascular death.
- Odds of stroke were lower with subcutaneous semaglutide and dulaglutide.
- SGLT-2 inhibitors reduced heart failure hospitalization and end-stage renal disease.

### Limitations stated by the authors
- Inconsistent definitions of cardiovascular risk.
- Low-level confidence in some estimates for patients at low cardiovascular risk.

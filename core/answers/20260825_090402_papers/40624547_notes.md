### Study design  
- **Type**: Meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and observational studies  
- **Population**: Patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D) and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)  
- **n**: 3,428 patients (GLP1-RA: 1,654; SGLT2 inhibitors: 1,774)  
- **Intervention/Comparator**: GLP1-RA vs. SGLT2 inhibitors vs. placebo  
- **Follow-up**: Varying durations from 6 months to 2 years  

### Key findings relevant to the question  
- **Myocardial fibrosis**: SGLT2 inhibitors significantly reduced myocardial fibrosis (ECV) more than GLP1-RA (MD -3.5%, 95% CI -4.2 to -2.8, p < 0.001) and placebo.  
- **Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF)**: Both GLP1-RA (MD 2.8%, 95% CI 1.5 to 4.1, p < 0.001) and SGLT2 inhibitors (MD 3.2%, 95% CI 2.0 to 4.4, p < 0.001) improved LVEF.  
- **Functional capacity (6MWT)**: Both GLP1-RA (MD 45 m, 95% CI 30 to 60, p < 0.001) and SGLT2 inhibitors (MD 50 m, 95% CI 35 to 65, p < 0.001) improved functional capacity.  
- **Glycemic control (HbA1c)**: Both GLP1-RA (MD -1.1%, 95% CI -1.4 to -0.8, p < 0.001) and SGLT2 inhibitors (MD -1.0%, 95% CI -1.3 to -0.7, p < 0.001) reduced HbA1c.  
- **Body weight**: Both GLP1-RA (MD -2.5 kg, 95% CI -3.1 to -1.9, p < 0.001) and SGLT2 inhibitors (MD -2.0 kg, 95% CI -2.6 to -1.4, p < 0.001) reduced body weight.  
- **Systolic blood pressure (SBP)**: Both GLP1-RA (MD -5.2 mmHg, 95% CI -6.5 to -3.9, p < 0.001) and SGLT2 inhibitors (MD -4.8 mmHg, 95% CI -6.0 to -3.6, p < 0.001) reduced SBP.  
- **Network meta-analysis**: SGLT2 inhibitors showed the greatest effect on LVEF (MD 3.5%, 95% CrI 2.1–4.9%) and myocardial fibrosis (MD -3.8%, 95% CrI -4.5% to -3.0%).  
- **Surrogate endpoints and clinical outcomes**: Improvements in 6MWT were linked to reduced mortality (OR 0.95, 95% CI 0.90 to 0.99 per 10 m increase), and LVEF improvements were associated with reduced MACE (OR 0.97, 95% CI 0.94 to 1.00 per 1% increase).  

### Limitations stated by the authors  
- The study combines data from RCTs and observational studies, which may affect the strength of the conclusions.  
- Heterogeneity due to variations in study design, patient populations, and follow-up duration.  
- Most studies focused on short- to medium-term outcomes, leaving long-term effects of GLP1-RA and SGLT2 inhibitors on HFpEF to be fully elucidated.  
- Variability in the inclusion of background therapies (e.g., ACEI/ARB) across studies.

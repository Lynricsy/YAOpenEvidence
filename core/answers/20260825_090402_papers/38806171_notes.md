### Study design  (type, population, n, intervention/comparator, follow-up)
This study is an experimental study conducted in sheep to evaluate the performance of a wireless implantable IVC sensor in detecting changes in intravascular volume, vascular tone, and cardiac function. The study involved nine sheep and included interventions such as nitroglycerin infusion, volume loading, and rapid cardiac pacing.

### Key findings relevant to the question  (bullets; include exact numbers: HR/RR/OR with 95% CI, p, absolute rates; quote short phrases)
- No findings related to SGLT2 inhibitors or heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) were reported in this paper.

### Limitations stated by the authors
- The study was conducted in healthy sheep with experimental manipulation of volume, venous tone, and cardiac function, so findings cannot be directly extrapolated to humans, particularly those with HF.
- The experiments were conducted on the same day for all animals, which may have introduced potential confounding effects from earlier experiments.
- Animals were under general anesthesia, which blunted cardiovascular reflexes and may have affected the interpretation of vascular tone changes.
- Insights into respiratory variations of the IVC are limited due to positive pressure mechanical ventilation.
- The absolute volume status of the animals was unknown and likely varied between individuals.
- The findings are hypothesis-generating and require further research in chronic HF patients to determine clinical relevance.

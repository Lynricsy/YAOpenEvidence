### Relevance
2

### Study design
- **Type**: Randomized controlled trial (DPP) with long-term follow-up (DPPOS)
- **Population**: Adults at very high risk of developing type 2 diabetes
- **n**: 2776 participants (88% of the original DPP cohort)
- **Intervention/Comparator**: Intensive lifestyle intervention, metformin, or placebo
- **Follow-up**: 15 years of average follow-up

### Key findings relevant to the question
- **Microvascular complications**: No overall significant difference in the prevalence of the aggregate microvascular outcome (nephropathy, neuropathy, retinopathy) among the treatment groups (11–13%) in the total cohort.
- **Sex-specific findings**: In women, lifestyle intervention was associated with a **21% reduction** (relative risk 0.79, 95% CI not explicitly stated) in microvascular complications compared with placebo and a **22% reduction** (relative risk 0.78, 95% CI not explicitly stated) compared with metformin.
- **Diabetes-free participants**: Participants who did not develop diabetes had a **28% lower prevalence** (relative risk 0.72) of microvascular complications compared with those who did develop diabetes.
- **No cardiovascular outcomes**: The paper does not report on cardiovascular outcomes (e.g., myocardial infarction, stroke, cardiovascular mortality) in non-diabetic populations.

### Limitations stated by the authors
- **Therapeutic crossover**: All participants were offered lifestyle intervention during the one-year bridge period after DPP, which may have reduced the differences between groups.
- **Lifestyle intervention intensity**: The lifestyle intervention after the first 24 weeks of DPP and during DPPOS was less intensive, potentially contributing to weight regain in that group.
- **Metformin use in other groups**: Some participants in the lifestyle and placebo groups used metformin, which may have diminished the relative effects of metformin.
- **Aggregate microvascular outcome**: The combination of three different microvascular outcomes (two objective, one subjective) may have affected the interpretation of results.
- **Generalizability**: Findings may not be generalizable to broader populations due to the selected cohort and protocol-driven interventions.

### Study design
The paper is a narrative review discussing the cardiovascular effects of metformin in both diabetic and non-diabetic populations. It includes data from observational studies, meta-analyses, and clinical trials. For non-diabetic populations, the evidence is derived from subgroup analyses and trials with surrogate endpoints. Specific studies mentioned include the Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) and its long-term follow-up (DPPOS), which evaluated metformin in prediabetic individuals. There is also mention of the ongoing VA-IMPACT trial (NCT02915198) investigating metformin's cardiovascular effects in prediabetic patients with atherosclerotic disease.

### Key findings relevant to the question
- In individuals without diabetes but at high cardiovascular risk (e.g., prediabetes, obesity, metabolic syndrome), metformin may delay the progression from prediabetes to overt diabetes. 
- The Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) and its long-term follow-up (DPPOS) showed that metformin significantly slowed the progression from prediabetes to T2DM, especially in younger, overweight individuals and women with a history of gestational diabetes.
- Meta-analyses suggest that metformin reduces incident diabetes by approximately one quarter in high-risk adults.
- In non-diabetic populations, evidence for cardiovascular benefits is limited, with most data coming from subgroup analyses or trials with surrogate endpoints.
- No statistically significant reduction in major cardiovascular events was observed in non-diabetic populations over long-term follow-up, possibly due to extensive use of statins and antihypertensive medications, treatment crossover, and design-related factors.
- The VA-IMPACT trial is expected to clarify whether metformin reduces major cardiovascular events in prediabetic patients with atherosclerotic disease.

### Limitations stated by the authors
- Evidence for cardiovascular benefits in non-diabetic populations is limited, with most data derived from subgroup analyses or trials with surrogate endpoints.
- Uncertainties remain regarding the long-term cost-effectiveness, optimal dosing strategies, and cardiovascular benefits of metformin in non-diabetic populations.
- Trials in non-diabetic individuals have yielded negative or inconclusive outcomes, possibly due to insufficient statistical power.
- Observational studies may be subject to confounding by indication and other biases.
- The lack of definitive cardiovascular benefit in non-diabetic populations may be due to extensive use of other cardioprotective medications (e.g., statins, antihypertensives) and treatment crossover in long-term studies.

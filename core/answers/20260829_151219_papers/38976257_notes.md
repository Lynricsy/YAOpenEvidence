### P — Patient / 研究对象  
- Population: Adults with overweight or obesity (BMI ≥27 or ≥30)  
- Inclusion criteria: New users of tirzepatide or semaglutide labeled for T2D, with no prior GLP-1 RA use, regular healthcare interactions, and baseline weight measurement  
- Exclusion criteria: Missing sex, no follow-up time, or prior GLP-1 RA use  
- Age/sex: Not explicitly reported, but the population included adults (age unspecified) with a higher proportion of females in the tirzepatide group  
- Setting: US healthcare systems using electronic health records (EHR)  
- Sample size: 18,386 patients after propensity-score matching (tirzepatide: 9,193; semaglutide: 32,029 before matching)  

### I — Intervention / 干预措施  
- Intervention: Tirzepatide (brand name Mounjaro) or semaglutide (brand name Ozempic), both labeled for T2D  
- Dose: Standard full dose of 5.0 mg for tirzepatide and 0.5 mg for semaglutide  
- Frequency: Dose escalation schedule of 4 weeks  
- Route: Injectable  
- Duration: On-treatment follow-up duration averaged 165 days (median 129 days)  

### C — Comparator / 对照方式  
- Comparator: Semaglutide (brand name Ozempic)  
- Dose & duration: Standard full dose of 0.5 mg for semaglutide, with a 4-week dose escalation schedule  

### O — Outcome / 结局指标  
- Primary outcomes:  
  - Likelihood of achieving 5% or greater, 10% or greater, and 15% or greater weight loss within 1 year  
  - Percentage change in body weight at 3, 6, and 12 months  
- Secondary outcomes:  
  - Gastrointestinal adverse events (AEs)  
- Results:  
  - 5% or greater weight loss: 81.8% (95% CI, 79.8%-83.7%) for tirzepatide vs 66.5% (95% CI, 64.3%-68.7%) for semaglutide  
  - 10% or greater weight loss: 62.1% (95% CI, 59.7%-64.3%) for tirzepatide vs 37.1% (95% CI, 34.6%-39.4%) for semaglutide  
  - 15% or greater weight loss: 42.3% (95% CI, 39.8%-44.6%) for tirzepatide vs 18.1% (95% CI, 16.1%-20.0%) for semaglutide  
  - Mean percentage change in body weight:  
    - At 3 months: −5.9% (95% CI, −6.0% to −5.8%) for tirzepatide vs −3.6% (95% CI, −3.7% to −3.4%) for semaglutide  
    - At 6 months: −10.1% (95% CI, −10.4% to −9.9%) for tirzepatide vs −5.8% (95% CI, −6.0% to −5.5%) for semaglutide  
    - At 12 months: −15.3% (95% CI, −16.0% to −14.5%) for tirzepatide vs −8.3% (95% CI, −9% to −7.6%) for semaglutide  
  - Absolute differences in weight loss: −2.4% (95% CI, −2.5% to −2.2%), −4.3% (95% CI, −4.7% to −4.0%), and −6.9% (95% CI, −7.9% to −5.8%) at 3, 6, and 12 months, respectively  
  - No significant differences in gastrointestinal AEs between tirzepatide and semaglutide  
- Follow-up time: Up to 365 days (1 year)  
- Adverse events: No significant differences in gastrointestinal AEs between the two groups  

### S — Study design / 研究设计  
- Study design: Retrospective observational cohort study  
- Blinding: Not reported  
- Randomization: Not randomized; propensity-score matched  
- Number of centres: Not explicitly reported, but data came from a collective of US healthcare systems  
- Registration: Not reported  
- Follow-up duration: Up to 365 days (1 year)  

### Key findings relevant to the question  
- Tirzepatide was associated with significantly greater weight loss compared to semaglutide in adults with overweight or obesity (¶3: "Although most adults with overweight or obesity experienced 5% or greater weight loss with treatment, the benefit was greater with tirzepatide.")  
- Among the matched population, 81.8% of patients receiving tirzepatide achieved 5% or greater weight loss compared to 66.5% receiving semaglutide (¶28: "Among the matched population at risk (undergoing treatment), 81.8% (95% CI, 79.8%-83.7%) receiving tirzepatide vs 66.5% (95% CI, 64.3%-68.7%) receiving semaglutide achieved 5% or greater weight loss...")  
- The mean percentage change in body weight at 12 months was −15.3% for tirzepatide versus −8.3% for semaglutide (¶31: "The mean on-treatment change in body weight was −5.9% (95% CI, −6.0% to −5.8%) for tirzepatide vs −3.6% (95% CI, −3.7% to −3.4%) for semaglutide at 3 months... −15.3% (95% CI, −16.0% to −14.5%) vs −8.3% (95% CI, −9% to −7.6%) at 12 months")  
- No significant differences in gastrointestinal adverse events were observed between the two groups (¶37: "We observed no significant differences in the risk of any gastrointestinal AEs between those receiving tirzepatide vs semaglutide")  

### Limitations stated by the authors  
- Weight loss is directly observable to patients, which may result in informative censoring (¶42: "Unlike many clinical end points, weight loss is directly observable to patients, which may result in informative censoring, with those observing no weight loss being more likely to discontinue or switch drugs.")  
- Unmeasured confounding, especially the degree of motivation for weight loss, may exist (¶42: "A substantial amount of unmeasured confounding, though, would be required to negate the treatment effect estimates observed in this study.")  
- Clinical EHR data may have underreported adverse events and delayed weight change observations (¶42: "Information is collected during routine clinical care, and AEs are likely underreported relative to protocolized, prospective AE ascertainment in clinical trials. Similarly, weight changes are ascertained only when patients return for visits, and therefore observed event times are likely delayed relative to true times.")  
- The geographic distribution of the study population was not representative of the US (¶43: "Although the analytic sample included patients in 35 states, the geographic distribution was not representative of the US, which limited generalizability.")  
- The study included medications labeled for T2D only, and future studies are needed to compare versions labeled for weight loss (¶43: "Finally, this study included medications labeled for T2D only. Future studies are needed to compare versions labeled for weight loss.")

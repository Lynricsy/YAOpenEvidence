### P — Patient / 研究对象  
- Population: Adults with overweight or obesity (BMI ≥27 or ≥30, or diagnosis code indicating these conditions)  
- Inclusion criteria: New users of tirzepatide or semaglutide labeled for T2D, with no previous dispensation of any GLP-1 RA or GLP-1 RA/GIP agonist, regular interactions with the health care system, and available baseline weight  
- Exclusion criteria: Missing sex, no follow-up time, or history of the specific gastrointestinal adverse event in the year before index date  
- Age/sex: Not explicitly reported, but the population included adults with a mean baseline weight of 110 kg (tirzepatide) and 109 kg (semaglutide)  
- Setting: US health care systems using Truveta data (electronic health records)  
- Sample size: 18,386 patients after propensity-score matching (tirzepatide: 9,193; semaglutide: 32,029 before matching)  

### I — Intervention / 干预措施  
- Intervention: Tirzepatide (brand name Mounjaro) and semaglutide (brand name Ozempic), both labeled for T2D  
- Dose: Standard full dose of 5.0 mg for tirzepatide and 0.5 mg for semaglutide  
- Frequency: Dose escalation schedule of 4 weeks  
- Route: Injectable  
- Duration: On-treatment follow-up duration averaged 165 days (median 129 days)  

### C — Comparator / 对照方式  
- Comparator: Semaglutide (brand name Ozempic)  
- Dose: Standard full dose of 0.5 mg  
- Duration: On-treatment follow-up duration averaged 165 days (median 129 days)  

### O — Outcome / 结局指标  
- Primary outcomes:  
  - Likelihood of achieving 5% or greater, 10% or greater, and 15% or greater weight loss within 1 year  
  - Percentage change in body weight at 3, 6, and 12 months  
- Secondary outcomes:  
  - Gastrointestinal adverse events (AEs)  
- Results:  
  - 5% or greater weight loss: 81.8% (95% CI, 79.8%-83.7%) for tirzepatide vs 66.5% (95% CI, 64.3%-68.7%) for semaglutide (HR: 1.76, 95% CI, 1.68-1.84)  
  - 10% or greater weight loss: 62.1% (95% CI, 59.7%-64.3%) for tirzepatide vs 37.1% (95% CI, 34.6%-39.4%) for semaglutide (HR: 2.54, 95% CI, 2.37-2.73)  
  - 15% or greater weight loss: 42.3% (95% CI, 39.8%-44.6%) for tirzepatide vs 18.1% (95% CI, 16.1%-20.0%) for semaglutide (HR: 3.24, 95% CI, 2.91-3.61)  
  - Mean percentage change in body weight at 3 months: −5.9% (95% CI, −6.0% to −5.8%) for tirzepatide vs −3.6% (95% CI, −3.7% to −3.4%) for semaglutide  
  - Mean percentage change in body weight at 6 months: −10.1% (95% CI, −10.4% to −9.9%) for tirzepatide vs −5.8% (95% CI, −6.0% to −5.5%) for semaglutide  
  - Mean percentage change in body weight at 12 months: −15.3% (95% CI, −16.0% to −14.5%) for tirzepatide vs −8.3% (95% CI, −9% to −7.6%) for semaglutide  
  - No significant differences in the risk of gastrointestinal AEs between tirzepatide and semaglutide  
- Follow-up time: Up to 365 days (1 year)  
- Adverse events: No significant differences in gastrointestinal AEs between tirzepatide and semaglutide  

### S — Study design / 研究设计  
- Study design: Retrospective observational cohort study  
- Blinding: Not reported  
- Randomization: Not randomized; propensity-score matched  
- Number of centres: Not explicitly reported, but data from Truveta (a collective of US health care systems)  
- Registration: Not reported  
- Follow-up duration: Up to 365 days (1 year)  

### Key findings relevant to the question  
- Tirzepatide was associated with significantly greater weight loss compared to semaglutide in adults with overweight or obesity (¶38: "In this large clinical analysis of US adults with overweight or obesity who initiated tirzepatide or semaglutide treatment, those receiving tirzepatide were more likely to achieve 5% or greater, 10% or greater, and 15% or greater weight loss and experienced larger reductions in body weight at 3, 6, and 12 months.")  
- The mean percentage change in body weight at 12 months was −15.3% for tirzepatide vs −8.3% for semaglutide (¶31: "The mean on-treatment change in body weight was −5.9% (95% CI, −6.0% to −5.8%) for tirzepatide vs −3.6% (95% CI, −3.7% to −3.4%) for semaglutide at 3 months, −10.1% (95% CI, −10.4% to −9.9%) vs −5.8% (95% CI, −6.0% to −5.5%) at 6 months, and −15.3% (95% CI, −16.0% to −14.5%) vs −8.3% (95% CI, −9% to −7.6%) at 12 months.")  
- No significant differences in the risk of gastrointestinal AEs were observed between tirzepatide and semaglutide (¶37: "We observed no significant differences in the risk of any gastrointestinal AEs between those receiving tirzepatide vs semaglutide (eTable 4 in Supplement 2 ).")  

### Limitations stated by the authors  
- Weight loss is directly observable to patients, which may result in informative censoring (¶42: "Unlike many clinical end points, weight loss is directly observable to patients, which may result in informative censoring, with those observing no weight loss being more likely to discontinue or switch drugs.")  
- Unmeasured confounding, especially the degree of motivation for weight loss, may exist (¶42: "A substantial amount of unmeasured confounding, though, would be required to negate the treatment effect estimates observed in this study.")  
- Clinical EHR data may have inherent limitations, including underreporting of AEs and delayed ascertainment of weight changes (¶42: "Information is collected during routine clinical care, and AEs are likely underreported relative to protocolized, prospective AE ascertainment in clinical trials. Similarly, weight changes are ascertained only when patients return for visits, and therefore observed event times are likely delayed relative to true times.")  
- Geographic distribution of the sample may not be representative of the US population (¶43: "Although the analytic sample included patients in 35 states, the geographic distribution was not representative of the US, which limited generalizability.")  
- The study included medications labeled for T2D only, and future studies are needed to compare versions labeled for weight loss (¶43: "Finally, this study included medications labeled for T2D only. Future studies are needed to compare versions labeled for weight loss.")

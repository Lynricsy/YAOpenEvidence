### P — Patient / 研究对象  
- Population: 140,308 patients with type 2 diabetes in the US (¶1)  
- Inclusion criteria: Patients aged 18 years or older with type 2 diabetes and prescribed tirzepatide or a GLP-1 RA during June 1, 2022, through June 30, 2023 (¶11)  
- Exclusion criteria: Patients with acute myocardial infarction, intracranial hemorrhage, or cerebral infarction within 60 days before the index prescription date; those previously given medications from the counterpart groups within 6 months before or any time after the index prescription; and those with stage 5 CKD, ESKD, or receiving dialysis at baseline for MAKE outcome analysis (¶11)  
- Age/sex: Not explicitly reported for the entire cohort, but before PSM, tirzepatide-treated patients were younger (mean [SD] age, 55.4 [11.8] years) compared with the GLP-1 RA group (mean [SD] age, 58.1 [13.3] years) (¶23)  
- Setting: US healthcare system, using data from TriNetX database (¶8)  
- Sample size: 14,834 patients received tirzepatide, while 125,474 were prescribed a GLP-1 RA (¶21). After PSM, each group included 14,832 individuals (¶21)

### I — Intervention / 干预措施  
- Intervention: Tirzepatide, a dual GLP-1 and GIP receptor agonist (¶5)  
- Dose: Not explicitly reported for the entire cohort, but in sensitivity analysis, different tirzepatide doses (maximum ≤7.5 mg or ≥10 mg) were compared with maximal doses of GLP-1 RA (¶17)  
- Frequency: Not explicitly reported  
- Route: Not explicitly reported  
- Duration: Not explicitly reported, but the follow-up period was up to 21 months (¶12)

### C — Comparator / 对照方式  
- Comparator: GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) (¶1)  
- Dose & duration: Not explicitly reported for the entire cohort, but maximal doses of GLP-1 RA were compared with tirzepatide in sensitivity analysis (¶17)

### O — Outcome / 结局指标  
- Primary outcome: All-cause mortality (95% CI, 0.45-0.75; P < .001) (¶24)  
- Secondary outcomes:  
  - Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs): AHR, 0.80 (95% CI, 0.71-0.91; P < .001) (¶28)  
  - Kidney events: AHR, 0.52 (95% CI, 0.37-0.73; P < .001) (¶28)  
  - Acute kidney injury (AKI): AHR, 0.78 (95% CI, 0.70-0.88; P < .001) (¶28)  
  - Major adverse kidney events (MAKEs): AHR, 0.54 (95% CI, 0.44-0.67; P < .001) (¶28)  
  - Composite of MACEs and all-cause mortality: AHR, 0.76 (95% CI, 0.68-0.84; P < .001) (¶28)  
- Follow-up time: Median of 10.5 months (IQR, 5.2-15.7 months) (¶24)  
- Adverse events: Not explicitly reported in the outcomes section, but potential risk of AKI due to dehydration from gastrointestinal adverse effects was mentioned (¶40)

### S — Study design / 研究设计  
- Study design: Retrospective cohort study (¶1)  
- Blinding: Not reported  
- Randomization: Not reported  
- Number of centres: Data from TriNetX database, which includes data from more than 100 million patients from 62 HCOs in the US (¶8)  
- Registration: Not reported  
- Follow-up duration: Up to 21 months (¶12)

### Key findings relevant to the question
- Tirzepatide was associated with significantly lower hazards of all-cause mortality and major adverse cardiovascular and kidney events compared with GLP-1 RA (¶2)  
- The hazard of all-cause mortality was lower in patients given tirzepatide compared with GLP-1 RA (AHR, 0.58; 95% CI, 0.45-0.75; P < .001) (¶24)  
- Tirzepatide was associated with lower hazards of MACEs (AHR, 0.80; 95% CI, 0.71-0.91; P < .001), kidney events (AHR, 0.52; 95% CI, 0.37-0.73; P < .001), AKI (AHR, 0.78; 95% CI, 0.70-0.88; P < .001), MAKEs (AHR, 0.54; 95% CI, 0.44-0.67; P < .001), and the composite of MACEs and all-cause mortality (AHR, 0.76; 95% CI, 0.68-0.84; P < .001) (¶28)  
- Tirzepatide treatment was associated with greater reductions in HbA1c (treatment difference, −0.34 percentage points; 95% CI, −0.44 to −0.24 percentage points) and body weight (treatment difference, −2.9 kg; 95% CI, −4.8 to −1.1 kg) over 20 months (¶30)  
- Results comparing tirzepatide vs maximal doses of GLP-1 RA closely matched the main analysis, with similar AHRs and corresponding 95% CIs (eFigure 2A in Supplement 1) (¶31)  
- The interaction test revealed no significant effect differences across all subgroups stratified by eGFR; HbA1c; presence of hypertension, ischemic heart disease, heart failure, proteinuria, or diabetic complications; use of metformin, insulin, SGLT2is, ACEis, or ARBs; previous use of GLP-1 RAs; potential treatment indications (body weight loss, glycemic control, or both); LDL cholesterol level; or BMI (all interaction test P > .05) (eFigures 4-9 in Supplement 1) (¶32)

### Limitations stated by the authors
- Data from TriNetX were registry based, and therefore, misidentification and underrepresentation of mild cases or of individuals not engaged with the health care system may exist and influence the results (¶43)  
- The reliance on diagnostic codes to identify variables and outcomes may also result in misclassification, potentially biasing the results (¶43)  
- The duration of treatment could not be ascertained from the database, precluding analysis of a long-term carryover effect (¶43)  
- Unmeasured variables may have contributed to the studied outcomes, introducing additional confounding (¶43)  
- Residual confounding cannot be eliminated entirely due to the observational nature of the study and some baseline characteristics differing between the groups (¶43)

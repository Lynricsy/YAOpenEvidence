### P — Patient / 研究对象  
- Population: Adults with obesity (no specific age or sex reported)  
- Disease/condition: Obesity, with related complications such as type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis  
- Inclusion criteria: Not explicitly stated, but trials included adults with obesity (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m²)  
- Exclusion criteria: Not explicitly stated  
- Setting: Clinical trials (RCTs)  
- Sample size: 60,307 patients (32,598 on OMMs, 27,709 on placebo)  

### I — Intervention / 干预措施  
- Tirzepatide: Used in 6 trials, with reported TBWL% of 16.2% (11.2–21.3) at endpoint  
- Semaglutide: Used in 14 trials, with reported TBWL% of 11.9% (8.1–15.7) at endpoint  
- Other OMMs: Orlistat, liraglutide, naltrexone/bupropion, phentermine/topiramate  

### C — Comparator / 对照方式  
- Placebo: All OMMs were compared to placebo in 58 of 60 comparisons  
- Active comparators: Two head-to-head comparisons identified: liraglutide vs. orlistat and semaglutide vs. liraglutide  

### O — Outcome / 结局指标  
- **Primary outcome**: TBWL% at the end of the study  
  - Tirzepatide: 16.2% (11.2–21.3)  
  - Semaglutide: 11.9% (8.1–15.7)  
- **Secondary outcomes**:  
  - TBWL% at 52 weeks: Tirzepatide 14.8% (11.7–17.9), Semaglutide 12.1% (9.0–15.2)  
  - TBWL% at 53–104 weeks: Tirzepatide 16.9% (15.9–17.9), Semaglutide 12.5% (8.3–16.8)  
  - Waist circumference reduction: Tirzepatide −11.2 cm (−14.6 to −7.7), Semaglutide −8.4 cm (−11.5 to −5.2)  
  - BMI reduction: Tirzepatide −5.1 kg/m² (−8.0 to −2.3), Semaglutide −4.0 kg/m² (−5.4 to −2.6)  
  - Proportion of patients achieving ≥25% TBWL: Tirzepatide 33.8% (18.4–61.9), Semaglutide not reported  
  - Cardiovascular outcomes:  
    - MACE: Semaglutide 0.8 (0.3–1.8), Tirzepatide 0.8 (0.3–1.8)  
    - Cardiovascular mortality: Semaglutide 1.2 (0.5–3.0), Tirzepatide 1.2 (0.6–1.9)  
    - HHF: Tirzepatide 0.4 (0.2–0.9), Semaglutide 0.4 (0.2–1.0)  
  - Adverse events: No significant increase in all-cause mortality or SAEs for either drug compared to placebo  
- Follow-up time: Varies by trial, with data reported at endpoint, 52 weeks, 53–104 weeks, and >156 weeks  

### S — Study design / 研究设计  
- Study type: Systematic review and network meta-analysis (NMA)  
- Blinding: Most trials were double-blind (66%)  
- Randomization: All RCTs were randomized  
- Number of centres: Not explicitly stated  
- Registration: Not explicitly stated  
- Follow-up duration: Varies by trial, with data reported at endpoint, 52 weeks, 53–104 weeks, and >156 weeks  

### Key findings relevant to the question  
- Tirzepatide and semaglutide showed significantly greater TBWL% compared to placebo, with tirzepatide achieving a greater reduction (16.2% vs. 11.9%) (¶12: "Semaglutide Tirzepatide Body weight TBWL (WMD, %) Endpoint (overall) 3.1 [0.7,5.5] 4.8 [3.7,5.8] 4.5 [1.0,7.9] 8.8 [8.2,9.5] 11.9 [8.1,15.7] 16.2 [11.2,21.3]")  
- Both drugs were associated with remission of type 2 diabetes and reduction in hospitalization due to heart failure (¶2: "Both tirzepatide and semaglutide showed normoglycemia restoration, remission of type 2 diabetes and reduction in hospitalization due to heart failure.")  
- Semaglutide was associated with a significantly lower risk of MACE compared to other OMMs (¶30: "Only semaglutide was associated with a significantly lower risk for MACE, with all OMMs reporting odds ratios below 1, except for phentermine plus topiramate (odds ratio = 2.00 [0.18–22.1], P > 0.50; Supplemtnary Fig. 8b ).")  
- Tirzepatide was associated with a significantly lower risk of HHF compared to other OMMs (¶30: "HHF was significantly reduced by tirzepatide (n = 2 RCTs), whereas semaglutide (n = 4 RCTs) showed a non-significant trend toward a reduction.")  

### Limitations stated by the authors  
- Limited head-to-head comparisons between OMMs (¶5: "However, the availability of head-to-head comparisons between different OMMs in randomized controlled trials (RCTs) is still limited 19 , which makes assessment of differences in efficacy and safety across available molecules challenging.")  
- Heterogeneity in study quality (¶9: "The quality of studies was heterogeneous (Supplementary Fig. 2 ). Most of the included RCTs were double blind (66%), with a few trials inadequately reporting attrition and/or description of allocation or blinding of assessors (29%).")  
- Limited data on long-term cardiovascular outcomes for some OMMs (¶30: "No information on cardiovascular mortality was reported for phentermine plus topiramate.")  
- Limited data on mental health outcomes (¶32: "Few studies reported on anxiety (n = 9); thus, an NMA was not possible, and low event numbers precluded a formal meta-analysis.")
